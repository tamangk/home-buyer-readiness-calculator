Your plan to get ready

    Good to know before you choose

    Conventional

    The standard loan, and usually the cheapest over time once your credit is strong.

    • 3 percent down for first time buyers, 5 percent for most others, 20 percent removes mortgage insurance entirely.
    • Mortgage insurance can be removed once you reach 20 percent equity. FHA insurance cannot, unless you refinance.
    • Needs a credit score of 620 or higher with most lenders, and rewards scores above 740 with the best pricing.
    • Loan limit of $832,750 in 2026.

    FHA

    The flexible loan, built for buyers whose credit or savings are still catching up.

    • 3.5 percent down with a score of 580 or higher. Scores of 500 to 579 need 10 percent down.
    • Allows higher debts relative to income than Conventional, up to about 56.9 percent with strong compensating factors.
    • Adds an upfront mortgage insurance premium of 1.75 percent, rolled into the loan, plus about 0.55 percent per year.
    • Kent County loan limit of $541,287. The home must pass an FHA appraisal with health and safety checks.

    MSHDA, Michigan help

    State help for first time buyers, and for repeat buyers in targeted areas, through participating lenders.

    • MI 10K provides up to $10,000 toward down payment and closing costs, with no monthly payment.
    • The help is a second loan with zero interest, repaid when you sell, refinance, or pay off the first loan. It is not forgiven.
    • Needs a credit score of 640 or higher, a home price of $566,355 or less, and household income within MSHDA limits for your county and household size.
    • You bring at least 1 percent of the price from your own funds, and you complete a homebuyer education course.
